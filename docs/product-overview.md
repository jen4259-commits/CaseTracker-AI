# CaseTracker-AI Product Overview

## Purpose

CaseTracker-AI is a case research, verification, and media-production workflow. It helps a human-led team turn a compelling legal or public-interest case into a documented, source-traceable episode.

It is more than a case database: the product connects research, evidence handling, story planning, production, review, and export while preserving the link between published claims and their sources.

## Mission

**Research → Verify → Select → Build narrative → Edit → Review → Publish**

## Users

- Researchers who discover cases and collect records
- Fact-checkers who evaluate claims, sources, and conflicts
- Producers and editors who plan narration and media
- Reviewers who approve factual accuracy, rights, privacy, and final exports

## Product workflow

1. **Intake:** create a case record, record why it is a candidate, and collect leads.
2. **Research:** gather attributable records, reporting, interviews, and media. Record provenance and access details.
3. **Verification:** connect claims to sources, assess support and conflicts, and route unresolved items for human review.
4. **Story design:** build a dated timeline, outline, narration plan, and storyboard from reviewed material.
5. **Production:** track media assets, rights/permissions, timing, narration, overlays, and edit status.
6. **Review:** check factual accuracy, context, privacy, legal/ethical considerations, audio, timing, and branding.
7. **Export:** record the approved master and distribution variants, then preserve an archive and traceability report.

## Pilot 1: Verified Case to Finished Episode

Pilot 1 validates the complete workflow with one selected case and a target episode runtime of approximately 30 minutes. Deliverables include a case brief, source log, verified timeline and fact sheet, asset inventory, narration script, storyboard, final branded episode, publish-ready exports, and source traceability report.

The pilot is complete only after a human reviewer approves the factual, editorial, rights, and export checks. The software must not imply that a source or claim is verified merely because an automated agent processed it.

## Trust, safety, and editorial principles

- Every material factual claim must link to one or more sources and a specific supporting passage, timestamp, or record location where possible.
- Preserve source identity, retrieval date, original URL or archive reference, and any transformations made to media.
- Distinguish allegations, opinions, findings, and established facts; record procedural posture and the date information was checked.
- Flag conflicts and uncertainty rather than silently resolving them. Human review is required for material conflicts and high-impact claims.
- Do not represent a search as exhaustive. Research coverage and its limits must be documented.
- Track licenses, permissions, restrictions, and attribution. Do not treat public availability as permission to reuse copyrighted material.
- Minimize unnecessary personal or sensitive information, particularly for private individuals and minors.
- Preserve originals where lawful and appropriate; keep edited derivatives traceable to their source.
- Require human approval before publication. Automated tools assist; they do not make editorial or legal determinations.

## Initial product capabilities

- Case intake and status tracking
- Source catalog and verification log
- Claims, evidence, and timeline relationships
- Media asset metadata and rights tracking
- Episode outline, script, storyboard, and segment timing
- Review gates and approvals
- Export and archive records

## Out of scope for the first pilot

- Guaranteed discovery of every website or source
- Autonomous publication
- Automated conclusions about guilt, innocence, credibility, or legal outcomes
- Downloading or republishing media without permission or a lawful basis
- Fully automated long-form editing without human review

## Pilot success measures

- One case completes the workflow from intake to approved export.
- All material claims in the episode are traceable to reviewed sources.
- Conflicts, uncertainty, source limitations, and media rights are recorded.
- Reviewers can reproduce the episode's evidence trail from the archive.
- The workflow can be reused without losing provenance or review history.
