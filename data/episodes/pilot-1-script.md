## Narration draft: opening (approx. 1–2 minutes)

**HOST:** What happens when a person accused of a serious crime asks a court for a lawyer—and the answer is no? In 1963, the United States Supreme Court answered a constitutional question raised by Clarence Earl Gideon, a Florida defendant who could not afford counsel.

The case is *Gideon v. Wainwright*. The Court's opinion describes Gideon's request for appointed counsel, the denial of that request, and his decision to represent himself at trial. The legal question was bigger than one courtroom: whether the Constitution required states to provide counsel to defendants charged with serious crimes who could not afford an attorney.

This episode follows the record—not rumor. We will distinguish what the Supreme Court held from what later summaries say about the retrial, and we will identify where the documentary record still needs checking. A right to counsel is not the same as a guarantee of a particular outcome.

**ON SCREEN:** *Gideon v. Wainwright*, 372 U.S. 335 (1963). Source: U.S. Reports, Library of Congress. Pinpoint review required before final cut.

## Narration draft: the holding (approx. 2–3 minutes)

**HOST:** In *Gideon v. Wainwright*, the Supreme Court concluded that the right to counsel is fundamental and applies to state felony defendants through the Fourteenth Amendment. The opinion explains why a lawyer matters in a criminal trial: the process is complex, and an accused person may be unable to defend themselves effectively without legal assistance.

The holding is important, but it should be stated precisely. This case concerned a felony prosecution in state court. It did not say that appointed counsel is required in every legal proceeding, and it did not decide whether Gideon was guilty or innocent of the original charge. The Court reversed the conviction and sent the case back for further proceedings; that is the legal result established by the Supreme Court opinion.

**ON SCREEN:** “A constitutional right to counsel in state felony cases.” Cite: *Gideon*, 372 U.S. 335, 342–345. Verify exact pinpoint and quotation wording against the opinion before final publication.
